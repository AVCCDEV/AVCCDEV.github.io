Cinematic 3rdPerson Game

  • Project College project
  • Tools
    • Unreal Engine
    • Maya

I was inspired by a game about a made up god tustling with other gods to try making my own seamless cutscene to gameplay transition system. This was my first foray into making cinematic, locomotion, and attack systems for a game. All animations and systems made by me. All rigs and models gathered from the internet or personal friends.

Credit to Casey Mcdermott and Mauricio Hoffman for their mentorship in game animation and Taylor Whitset for his mntorship in Unreal Animation systems.

Cutscene Animation

WiP Animation Sequence

The camera work and character animation for the cutscene was handled completely in Maya an then blended together with the gameplay.

Though the cutscene is a self contained sequence, I decided to animate portions of the sequence as discrete animations and cycles that would then be combined into the final cutscene. Working modularly helped keep up the pace of production while also keeping animation assets on hand for reuse in gameplay later.

Cutscene to Gameplay Blueprint

The Blueprint For the Cutscene to Gameplay Transition System can be viewed here (ctrl+Scroll to zoom, ctrl+RightClick+Drag to pan)

For Debug, I made a gui to provide the choice of playing the cutscene or jumping straight into gameplay. If the cutscene is chosen then the event to start the cutscene will be called

I originally tried to make the character's traversal through space dependant on Unreal's built in Root Motion system. This proved to provide very finnacky results, however. I was able to solve this with a method that nulifies the character's movement system and teleports the character actor to an attach point on the root of an anim instance for the cutscene that is located in world space. I took to calling them "RootMotion Dummies"

When the character and camera is successfully teleported to the root motion dummy the character bp would spawn and play a level sequence for the cutscene.

Because I wanted to use cinecameras for the character follow camera in order to control depth of field and exposure during the cutscene I made a function called Sequence Duration to playrate. This way whatever settings I use that are not directly controlled by the sequence would only last for as long as the sequence does, plus or minus an arbitrary length of time.

Setting things up this way allowed me to control when in the sequence to reset the player controller, handle updating post process settings like DOF, resetting the player controller, and when to destroy the level sequence. This step was also important as it would allow me to notify the Ystra blueprint when the cutscene was over, so the enemy AI could also transition to gameplay.

When the cutscene was over whats left was to detatch the Actors from their rootmotion dummys and attach the camera back to the player character's camera boom. Then control is given back to the player with the player's collision reenabled.

What comes next is resetting the camera to a predetermined focal length and arm length. I did this because I wanted a subtly gamey way of signalling to the player that they could control the character so I also added a feature to control transitioning to "gamecam".

Parameters such as how far away the camera is boomed out from the player, ending focal length, and the rate of the transition are controllable in the characer instance by default, but can also be set on a per sequence basis.

For the cutscene animation itself, transitioning in and out of the animation is controlled by a variable called BlendInterp that is set by the ThirdPersonCharacterBP and casted to the animBPs of the other elements in the scene affected by the sequencer such as Mjolnir and Ystra.

This way each affected animBP could blend between the Cutscene slot and the regular gameplay animation based on one master variable keyable in the sequencer.

When the character has control over the camera again, another blueprint script takes over the gamecam in order to offset the camera to the side when looking up

Gameplay Animation

Locomotion

I decided to keep the locomotion statemachine rather basic in order to accomodate the rest of the complexity of the project as well as to challenge myself to see how much I could get out of a simple idle to run blend.

In order to give the player character a better feel to the locomotion, I have added a feature to allow for the player to move faster going forward than going backward. This is reflected in the form of a curve in the placement of animation cycles in the blendspace.

In the animation Blueprint, I setup two different configurations. One to take the blendspace for when the Thor's default movement, and another to handle when Thor is aiming. I found that when aiming and using Fullbody IK Stride Warping sometimes Thor would have a tendancy to have a squatted down duckwalk. This was due to the fact that the hammer swing animations were from the more rooted idle pose. To correct this I set up bone transforms to tweak the pelvis position relative to the ik-feet.

The Locomotion Animations Were handled largely with a 2D blendspace. this allowed me to get cover a large number of animation possibilities while only really animating movement going in one direction and mirroring over the animations to account for the other direction.

In order to cover a wide variety of directional movement, I made 5 strafing variants. A horizontal run, diagonal run, cross-over run, diagonal sidestep run, and sidestep run. In gameplay they provide a naturalistic movement without foot intersections and blend well with the idle.

In order to add variety and weight while saving on animation work I programmed a procedural torso lean into the animation blueprint. this way, identical animation assets played in reverse or mirrored will have a plausible weightshift to them based on the player pawn's direction. I had added values aswell that can tweak how much Thor would lean in any given direction

A similar function using different input variables drives how the torso would lean based on the camera aim, as well as how thor tilts his hammer ever so slightly to leave room for the player to aim

Combat

The state machine for the hammer throw attack has the animBP playing an anim montage to enter and exit from three hold states, The cached locomotion pose, the hammer swing cycle, and the RecallHold.

The Blueprint For the combat systems can be viewed here on compatible devices (on pc, ctrl+Scroll to zoom, ctrl+RightClick+Drag to pan)

The ranged attack is a simple aim-throw-recall gameplay loop. The aiming is rather basic, when the character holds aim, the camera zoom and dollys in to a desired focal length and boom arm length, when the aim is released it returns.

The attack checks if the player is currently holding the hammer and also aiming. If the player is, then when the blueprint recieves the notify from the anim montage, a line is traced from a socket on Thor's shoulder to whatever is at the center of camera view

The hammer in Thor's hand gets hidden and a new projectile hammer is spawned. It then travels from A to B at a rate defined by the designer. Because this is controlled with a time line the ramping of the speed can be controlled by the desiger as well.

Open the Hammer Projectile Blueprint ↗

The Hammer Recall is driven by a spline component created at run time. In order to tweak the spline I added variables to control the tangent handle. Because the recall is handled by a timeline, it can be tweaked just the same as the hammer throw.

I wanted to add a feature where the player could hit an object with the hammer on the recall if they were slick about it, but also wanted to control how easy or difficult is was for the player to do so. To manage this, while the hammer projectile is recalling, it checks if a compatible entity is in range of the path with a sphere trace.

If the projectile ends up detecting the enemy, its path will be nudged toward it. The Radius Can be tweaked as well

Ystra Enemy Ai

I kept things minimalistic with Ystra's Animation I kept the idle animation with some dynamics to add some inertia her limbs.

Ystra's Ai is similarly straight forward. All her movement is bound to a volume, and on a timer, Ystra will move to a random point in the volume and continue attacking with spear rain.

Open the Ystra Blueprint ↗

In order to bring some life to Ystra as an Enemy, I added a hit react wherin the actor would jitter, and make the overall movement more erratic. Her Movement overall also speeds up the more she is hit.
